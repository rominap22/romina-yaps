### RD/RDN notes
Adopt a Mediterranean diet.
- anti-inflammatory foods
- ginger, turmeric, cumin
- whole foods - veggies, fruits, lean protein
    - no fried food, fast foods, or added sugars
- fish oil supplement for reducing inflammation (Nordic Naturals is a good brand for Omega-3), olive oil, avocado oil
- add in collagen, can mix in yogurt
- probiotic supplement - look for multi-strain blends or single strains containing [Lactobacillus spp.], [Bifidobacterium spp.], or the beneficial yeast [Saccharomyces boulardii] (such as strain CNCM I-745)
- kefir, Greek yogurt (170g)
- Seed is a good brand for probiotic supplement
- chicken, iron, Vitamin C (e.g. bell peppers, broccoli, tomatoes)

### Mel Robbins / Dr. Rajsree interview notes
Four systems of the gut garden:
1. motility - "irrigation system"; things should be moving through your gut move without getting slowed down or backed up
    - to promote motility, ensure your bowels are 100% and complete every day (avoiding constipation)
        - magnesium at bedtime (e.g. magnesium citrate, magnesium glycinate, magnesium malate)
            - magnesium citrate is a natural osmotic laxative - pulls water into your colon so that once you take it at bedtime, and when you wake up, your bowels go through
        - prokinetic - activates cleaning waves in the gut (e.g. ginger)
2. pollinators - "bees/butterflies"; need sufficient digestive enzymes, stomach acid, and bile to break down food
    - high-potency digestive enzymes before or after meals to help break down food (proteins, fat, carbs) better so you do not get bloated
    - natural ways to break down food - water with lemon juice or apple cider vinegar
3. garden bed - "soil"; integrity of gut lining (need to not have inflammation to avoid leaky gut), 70% of immune cells are sitting under gut lining
4. microbiome - "plants and weeds"; good and bad bacteria, need a balance and variety of good bacteria so there is no imbalance of good and bad bacteria (aka dysbiosis)
